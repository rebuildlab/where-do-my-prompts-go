Air in IDEs
Air in IDEs
is a
plugin
for agentic development. It gives you one place to start coding agents, follow what they are doing, and review the changes they make.
Air in IDEs
is not
an AI service of its own: it is a workspace for running coding agents. Agents come either from your existing local installations or from the
agent registry
. Depending on the agent and your setup, you can use JetBrains AI, a third-party provider account, or the agent's own authorization flow.

Why use Air in IDEs
Air in IDEs works with the agents, subscriptions, and IDE setup you already have:
Agents you already have installed
Air in IDEs detects the coding agents installed on your machine, such as Codex or Junie, the same way the IDE detects SDKs and build tools. A detected agent keeps the configuration it already uses.
Your existing subscriptions
Use the provider accounts and subscriptions you already have, or configure a provider in Air in IDEs. A JetBrains AI subscription is supported but not required.
IDE tools for agent changes
Agent sessions run in the same IDE as the rest of your work. Use code insight, refactorings, the debugger, and version control on the code an agent produces, and review, edit, or revert each change before you keep it.
Presets for different tasks
Save an agent, model, and operation mode as a named preset and reuse it instead of configuring each session. For example, keep one preset for small changes and another for larger, multi-step work.

How Air in IDEs works
Air in IDEs runs agents on your machine and communicates with ACP-compatible agents through the
Agent Client Protocol (ACP)
. CLI agents run in their own terminal interface. Because ACP is a standard interface, compatible agents can work without a dedicated integration.
Agents become available to Air in IDEs in two ways:
Local installations
â agents already installed on your machine. Air in IDEs detects them and makes them available in the IDE.
The ACP agent registry
â a catalog of ACP-compatible agents that you can browse and install with your consent. By default, Air in IDEs uses a JetBrains-provided registry.
A typical session looks like this:
You select an agent
for the task.
You authorize the agent
if it has no available authorization source yet.
You describe the task
in natural language.
The agent works in your project
, reading and editing files and running commands.
You review the result
and keep, edit, or revert the changes.
Data processing
Agents may be provided by JetBrains or third-party vendors. The agent or provider you choose determines how your requests and data are processed. When you use a third-party subscription, your code goes directly to that provider, not through JetBrains. The applicable terms and privacy policies also depend on the selected agent or provider. If you use JetBrains AI, the
JetBrains AI Platform Terms of Service
apply.
Air in IDEs does not enable AI features automatically and requires no login until you authorize an agent.
Control over AI in the IDE
Organizations can manage Air in IDEs centrally through
JetBrains Console
or
JetBrains IDE Services
and control which agents and providers are available to their users:
An administrator controls which agents, providers, models, and MCP servers are available and can apply different policies to different users and groups.
An allowlist of agents exposes only the agents that the organization approves. If an administrator turns off AI, the agent catalog is empty and the Air in IDEs user interface is hidden in the IDE, unless users have
agents of their own
.
An administrator can make the policies of the organization mandatory on managed user machines, so that users cannot bypass them with a personal JetBrains AI subscription or the AI of another organization.
For more information, refer to

Air in IDEs FAQ
What will happen to the existing AI Assistant plugin?
The existing
AI Assistant
plugin remains available and supported as a separate plugin. Air in IDEs is an experiment for evaluating a different approach to agentic development, and your feedback helps shape its direction.
How do I return to AI Assistant?
If you switched to Air in IDEs from AI Assistant, you can make AI Assistant available again without disabling Air in IDEs. For instructions, refer to
Return to AI Assistant

Which AI subscriptions and providers does Air in IDEs currently support?
Air in IDEs supports JetBrains AI for
compatible agents

A Claude subscription cannot be used to authorize Claude Agent in the chat

To use your Claude subscription, run Claude Code in the terminal surface, which Air in IDEs opens as a full-screen editor tab. For more information, refer to

How can I access Air in IDEs?
How you get Air in IDEs depends on the version of your IDE:
In 2026.2 versions of JetBrains IDEs, you need to install the Air in IDEs plugin from JetBrains Marketplace.
Starting from version 2026.3, the plugin is bundled with JetBrains IDEs, so you do not need to install it.
The plugin does not require a separate license. Depending on the agent and your setup, you can use JetBrains AI, a third-party provider account, or the existing agent configuration.
Can my organization turn off Air in IDEs?
Yes. The result depends on how your organization manages AI access:
If your organization manages AI access through IDE licenses and disables AI for the license assigned to you, Air in IDEs stays available only for agents of your own: custom ACP agents in
acp.json
and agents installed from the catalog, except Junie. These agents work only through their own authorization, and JetBrains AI and Junie are unavailable.
If you have no such agents, Air in IDEs is turned off. To use Air in IDEs, add an agent to
acp.json
and restart the IDE. For more information, refer to
Can I use custom ACP agents if AI is disabled in my license?
If your organization manages AI access through
JetBrains IDE Services
and disables
AI Enterprise
in the profile assigned to you, Air in IDEs is disabled in the same way.
If your organization manages AI access in JetBrains Console and turns it off for you, you lose access to the organization workspace: you can no longer spend its credits or start cloud runs. After you restart the IDE, the organization workspace no longer appears among the authorization sources. Air in IDEs does not switch to another authorization source automatically. Depending on the AI settings of your organization, other authorization methods, such as the own authorization of an agent or a third-party provider, can remain available.
To request access, contact your organization administrator.
For instructions on turning off Air in IDEs on specific machines or enforcing the policies of your organization, refer to

How do I turn off Air in IDEs?
Disable the Air in IDEs plugin in the IDE settings. For instructions, refer to
Disable Air in IDEs

Manage Air in IDEs on user machines

Which IDEs are supported?
The plugin is available in JetBrains IDEs except for Android Studio.
Does Air in IDEs work in China?
Yes. The plugin is distributed without geographic restrictions. However, due to
territorial restrictions
, JetBrains AI cannot be used as an authorization source in China. The availability of other agents and models is regulated by their respective vendors.
If the region of your IDE is set to
China Mainland
, the agent catalog lists only the agents that are available in China. Agents that are already installed on your machine stay available. To check the region, press
Ctrl+Alt+S
to open settings and select
Appearance & Behavior | System Settings | Language and Region
.
