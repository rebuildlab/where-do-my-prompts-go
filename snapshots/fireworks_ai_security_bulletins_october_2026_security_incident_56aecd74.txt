November 17, 2026
Update on October 2026 security incidents
This is an update on our investigation into the activity described in our November 9 and October 10 bulletins. We have determined this activity relates to two separate security incidents.
First incident (August 3 – October 7, 2026)
An unauthorized party probed and eventually gained unauthorized access to Fireworks systems. Using stolen Fireworks credentials and a vulnerability in one of our services, they misused our platform, and for a small number of customers, they accessed secrets, model files, and datasets. Unit 42 has completed its forensic investigation of this incident. This incident is contained and remediated.
Second incident (October 23 – October 30, 2026)
An unauthorized party gained access to Fireworks development machines hosted on a third-party cloud provider that had experienced a security incident and used that access to reach part of our production systems. The unauthorized party accessed account and billing information for all accounts created before Oct 30th, and, for a small number of customers, the unauthorized party accessed datasets, models, and/or secrets. Billing information does not include payment card numbers, which Fireworks does not store. AprosSyber is conducting a forensic investigation of this incident. This incident is also contained and remediated.
November 9, 2026
Update on October 2026 security incident
This is an update to our previous security bulletin published on October 9, 2026. In response to the incident, we notified law enforcement and obtained assistance from a leading cybersecurity firm, Palo Alto Networks Unit 42, to help conduct forensic analysis. At the time of publishing the bulletin, only Fireworks stored secrets (e.g. used in evaluation jobs) were known to have been accessed. Since then, we discovered impacts to account and billing information. For a limited set of accounts, we also discovered impact to secrets, datasets, and incomplete models.
The evidence indicates links to a cybercriminal threat actor group, which is financially motivated and aimed at selling tokens from unauthorized access to Large Language Models (LLMs) to illicit routing gateways, known as “LLMJacking”. On November 2, Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant published
a blog post
about the rise of LLMJacking, their motives, techniques used, and mitigation.
Further activity
A third-party cloud provider that hosted Fireworks R&D machines experienced a security incident on October 23. On October 24, the unauthorized third party used it to reach Fireworks development machines and then run an unauthorized workload that led to additional customer data impact. We detected this activity on the same day, and followed our incident response processes to take immediate remediation measures and close known critical vulnerabilities. As of October 25, we have not detected further evidence of successful unauthorized access by the threat actor on the Fireworks platform customers use.
Data involved
The affected internal application provided access to the following categories of information:
Resource metadata
, such as Fireworks resource identifiers, display names, descriptions, creation time, etc.
Billing information
, such as billing and shipping addresses, invoice and payment history, account balance, and credit usage. Payment card numbers are not stored by Fireworks and were not impacted.
Model weights and datasets
, for certain customers, a subset of incomplete model weights and datasets were accessed.
We are notifying customers whose data was impacted, and providing details about the impact and recommended response actions.
We have not detected any impact to
Inference
,
Training SDK
and
Nexus
, which are all zero data retention (ZDR) by default. These findings are based on our investigation to date. Our investigation is ongoing and may reveal additional information.
What we have done
Working with Unit 42, Fireworks identified and implemented security hardening procedures, including:
Access and credential lockdown
. Fireworks revoked and replaced credentials and service accounts, removed unnecessary elevated privileges, and tightened controls designed to prevent new unauthorized credentials from being created.
Network isolation
. Fireworks restricted unauthorized network pathways and isolated affected systems designed to prevent further unauthorized activity and movement between systems.
Data protection
. Fireworks reviewed and tightened access to data storage and analytics systems and enforced controls designed to prevent public or unauthorized access to stored data.
Forensic and audit integrity
. Fireworks secured and enhanced our logging and monitoring systems.
As part of our response, we’ve disabled self-serve dataset uploads and downloads used by Batch Inference and dataset-based training while we investigate this incident. Training SDK remains available with ZDR, i.e. no training data stored at rest, and Fireworks Lab-based training operates in an isolated environment with additional network and storage protection. We continue to work with Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 on our investigation and security hardening.
We will continue to monitor our systems 24/7 and will provide further updates on our investigation as appropriate.
October 10, 2026
Fireworks October 2026 security incident
On October 6, Fireworks identified a security incident involving unauthorized use of internal Fireworks credentials. An unauthorized third party accessed environment variables used in evaluation jobs in a limited number of customer accounts. Based on our investigation at this time, we have not detected evidence of unauthorized access to customer models, datasets, training data, personal information, or inference traffic within the Fireworks security perimeter or control.
We began containment the same day and have directly notified every identified affected customer. We are actively monitoring for further activity. We recognize the concern and work this creates for affected customers, and we are providing account-specific information to help them respond.
We’re working with a cybersecurity expert firm to assist with the incident.
Who is affected?
The incident affected a limited number of customer accounts. Affected customers received a direct email identifying the credentials requiring action.
If you have not received a notice, we have not identified your account as affected based on our investigation to date. If that changes, we will contact you directly.

What was affected?
We believe that environment variables, including API keys and tokens stored as secrets, were captured in certain affected accounts. Based on our investigation at this time, we have not detected evidence of unauthorized access to customer models, datasets, training data, inference traffic (including prompts and outputs), or personal information within the Fireworks security perimeter or control. Fireworks does not store prompts or outputs by default, as our platform is ZDR. We are continuing to review calls made with the exposed credentials to verify the scope, and our review of other potential access remains ongoing.
What we have done
We have revoked identified internal credentials used in the incident, affected Fireworks API keys, and keys identified as created by the actor, and added access restrictions. We are actively monitoring for further activity.
We are also reviewing how secrets are made available to evaluation jobs. We will share additional remediation measures as they are implemented and verified.
What affected customers should do
Replace revoked Fireworks API keys if you still need them.
The Fireworks API keys identified as revoked in your notice no longer work. Create new keys in your Fireworks account and update the applications that used the revoked keys. Those applications will fail authentication until updated.
Rotate third-party credentials identified in your notice.
Rotate or revoke them with the providers that issued them, then update the applications and Fireworks secrets that use them. Removing a stored secret alone does not invalidate the underlying credential. Third-party credentials require action with their issuing providers.
Review data and activity associated with exposed third-party credentials.
If your notice identifies third-party credentials, work with those providers to review access logs and data accessible using those credentials, including for unauthorized reads, downloads, or changes. Rotate the identified credentials even if you do not find suspicious activity, and follow the provider’s guidance if you identify unauthorized access.
Check for unrecognized resources or activity.
Review API keys, users, evaluators, training jobs, and datasets. Send us the names or IDs of anything you do not recognize so we can determine whether it is related and help you address it.
We can provide secret names and credential fingerprints through a secure channel to help you identify the exact credentials requiring rotation.
If you have not received an affected-customer notice, no incident-specific action is currently required. Contact us if you observe suspicious activity or need help confirming your account’s status.
